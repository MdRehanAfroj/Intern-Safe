Step 2 of 4

Common scam warning signs

One warning sign does not prove fraud. Verify claims independently before deciding.

₹

Upfront payment requests

Be cautious if someone asks for registration, security, equipment or training fees to secure an internship. Do not pay before independently checking the request.

✉

Unusual recruiter contact

Check whether the email domain and recruiter identity match contact details published on the company's official website. Similar-looking addresses can be misleading.

★

Unrealistic promises

Very high pay for little work, guaranteed selection, or pressure to accept immediately deserves closer scrutiny.

▤

Missing or vague details

Look for a clear role description, responsibilities, supervisor, duration and written terms. Independently confirm the organisation exists.

🔒

Requests for sensitive information

Do not share passwords, one-time codes or unnecessary banking/identity information during an unverified recruitment process.

Step 4 of 4

Your next steps

This prototype does not make a real-time or automated authenticity decision. Use the guidance below to decide what to verify next.

Review the opportunity carefully

Check the company and recruiter independently. If any details remain unclear, pause before accepting the offer, paying money or sharing sensitive information.

🛑

If payment is requested

Do not rush or pay just to secure the opportunity. Confirm the request directly with the organisation through an independently sourced contact.

📩

If you suspect fraud

Keep relevant messages and offer documents. Report the listing to the platform where you found it and seek guidance from your college or appropriate local authority.

🤝

If you are unsure

Ask your college placement cell or a trusted adviser to review the offer before proceeding.

This is educational guidance, not a guarantee that an internship is genuine or fraudulent. Verify through independent sources.
